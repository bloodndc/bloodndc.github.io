Questions donors ask us most

Straight answers, no medical jargon. If something here is unclear, write to us through the contact form.

Eligibility

Who can donate blood?
A healthy person aged 18–65, weighing at least 50 kg, with haemoglobin of 12.5 g/dL or more. The blood bank will check your blood pressure, temperature and haemoglobin before collecting anything.
When should I not donate?
Skip donation if you have a fever or infection, are taking antibiotics, had a tattoo or piercing in the last 6 months, had surgery recently, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or donated whole blood in the last 8 weeks. Tell the medical staff the truth — they will decide.
How often can I donate?
Men can donate whole blood every 3 months, women every 4 months. One Drop uses a 56-day cooling-off period, after which your listing automatically becomes available again.

Before and during donation

What should I do before donating?
Eat a normal meal — never donate on an empty stomach — and drink extra water. Sleep properly the night before, avoid heavy exercise that morning, and bring your national ID or birth certificate.
Does it hurt? Will I feel weak?
The prick lasts a few seconds and the collection takes about 8–10 minutes. You may feel light-headed for a short while; sitting for 10 minutes and drinking the juice they give you is usually enough. Your body replaces the plasma within 24 hours.
Is the equipment safe and reused?
No. Every donation uses a new, sterile, single-use kit that is opened in front of you. There is no risk of catching an infection from donating blood.

Using One Drop

Do I need an account to search for donors?
No. The directory, the emergency board and the compatibility tools are open to everyone. Only people who want to be listed as donors need to register — and even that needs no email or password.
Is my phone number visible to everyone?
No. Numbers stay masked. A requester has to tap Show contact on your card, and each browser is limited to 25 reveals per hour, which stops bulk scraping and cold-call spam.
How do I stop appearing in search results?
Open My donor profile on the device you registered from and tap Remove my listing. It takes effect immediately. You can also untick “available” to pause without deleting anything.
Why does “Nearest to me” sometimes look wrong?
Distance uses the donor’s shared location when they allowed it. If they did not, we fall back to the centre of their district, which is only an estimate. The card always shows the district so you can judge for yourself.
Does One Drop work without internet?
Once installed as an app, the interface and the last data you saw work offline. Posting a new request offline saves it on your device and syncs when you are back online.
Is this free? Who pays for it?
Completely free, with no ads and no sponsors. It is hosted on free student infrastructure and maintained by the Batch 27 Group 11 volunteers.

Medical disclaimer: One Drop is an information and coordination tool. It does not provide medical advice, does not screen donors and does not replace a hospital blood bank. Always follow the instructions of the treating doctor and the blood bank staff.

Quick checklist for donors

  • Ate a meal in the last 4 hours
  • Drank 2–3 glasses of water
  • Slept at least 6 hours
  • No fever, no antibiotics
  • At least 56 days since the last donation
  • National ID with you
