Questions donors ask us most

Clear answers without medical jargon. If anything remains unclear, write to us through the contact form — a person, not a bot, reads every message.

Eligibility

Who can donate blood?
In general: a healthy person aged 18–65, weighing at least 50 kg, with a haemoglobin level of 12.5 g/dL or above. You will not be left to guess — the blood bank checks your blood pressure, temperature and haemoglobin on site before anything is collected.
When should I not donate?
Postpone if you have a fever or active infection, are on antibiotics, had a tattoo or piercing in the past six months, had recent surgery, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or donated whole blood within the last eight weeks. Always answer the screening questions truthfully — the medical staff make the final call, and honesty protects the patient.
How often can I donate?
As a rule of thumb, men may donate whole blood every three months and women every four. One Drop enforces a 56-day cooling-off between donations; once it passes, your listing automatically becomes available again — no action needed from you.

Before and during donation

What should I do before donating?
Eat a normal meal (never donate on an empty stomach), drink extra water, and sleep properly the night before. Skip heavy exercise that morning and carry your national ID or birth certificate. Ten minutes of preparation makes the donation visibly easier.
Does it hurt? Will I feel weak?
The needle prick lasts a few seconds; the collection itself takes about 8–10 minutes. Some donors feel light-headed briefly — resting for ten minutes with the juice they provide is usually all it takes. Your body replaces the lost plasma within 24 hours.
Is the equipment safe and reused?
Yes — completely safe. Every donation uses a brand-new, sterile, single-use kit, opened in front of you and discarded afterwards. You cannot catch an infection by donating blood.

Using One Drop

Do I need an account to search for donors?
No. The directory, the emergency board and the compatibility tools are open to everyone, with no sign-up. Only people who want to be listed as donors register — and even that requires no email and no password.
Is my phone number visible to everyone?
No. Your number stays masked on your card. A requester must actively tap Show contact, and each browser is limited to 25 reveals per hour — a deliberate brake on bulk scraping and cold-call spam.
How do I stop appearing in search results?
Open My donor profile on the device you registered from and tap Remove my listing — it takes effect immediately. If you only need a break, use Mark as busy instead: you disappear from searches without losing your record.
Why does “Nearest to me” sometimes look wrong?
When a donor allowed location access, distance uses their shared coordinates. When they did not, we fall back to the centre of their district — an estimate, and labelled as such. The card always shows the district so you can judge for yourself.
Does One Drop work without internet?
Yes, within limits. Once installed as an app, the interface and the most recent data keep working without a connection. A request posted offline is stored on your device and syncs automatically the moment you are back online.
Is this free? Who pays for it?
Completely free — no ads, no sponsors, no fees hidden anywhere. It runs on free student infrastructure and is maintained in the spare time of the Batch 27, Group 11 volunteers.

Medical disclaimer: One Drop is an information and coordination tool. It does not provide medical advice, does not screen donors and does not replace a hospital blood bank. Always follow the instructions of the treating doctor and the blood bank staff.

Quick checklist for donors

  • Ate a meal in the last 4 hours
  • Drank 2–3 glasses of water
  • Slept at least 6 hours
  • No fever, no antibiotics
  • At least 56 days since the last donation
  • National ID with you
